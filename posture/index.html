2. Scope of this policy

This policy applies only to the “Posture Break” Android App distributed through the Google Play Store. It does not cover any other product, website, or third-party service except as expressly described here (in particular Google’s advertising, statistics and payment services). What the App is

The App reminds you, on a schedule you choose, to take a short break from sitting, and shows a short sequence of simple moves to do in that break. It counts the breaks you finish each day.

Posture Break is a wellness app. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition.

3. What data is processed

The App processes six separate categories of data, and a seventh arises only if you write to us. We keep them separate on purpose, because they behave very differently: one is kept on your device (3(a)), one is collected by Google for advertising and never reaches us (3(b)), one is a request for a static file, which sends none of your settings or break records but, like any web request, shows GitHub your IP address and ordinary connection information (3(c)), one is a small, predefined set of usage statistics (3(d)), one is the one-time Pro purchase, handled by Google Play (3(e)), one is a short list of what the App does not do (3(f)), and the last is the messages you send us (3(g)).

Health and fitness data. The App reads no sensor, takes no photo, measures nothing about your body and asks nothing about it. The facts about physical activity it sends, and only where the usage statistics are active, are that a reset started and from where, that “Another move” was used during one, and, in App version 1.0.0, a tap on a reminder’s Done button, which counts a break (Section 3(d)); Google Play’s data safety section lists them as fitness information. Like all the statistics, these facts are also available in AdMob (Section 3(d)). The count of breaks you finish each day is kept on your device, and in your phone’s own backup where that is switched on (Section 3(a)); it is never sent to us, to the usage statistics or to any advertiser. Which moves you did are never sent anywhere, and neither is whether you finished a reset. In App version 1.0.0, where the usage statistics are active, they record a tap on a reminder’s Done button, which is how a break is counted from a reminder, without your count of breaks or the move (Section 3(d)). These events show that this installation was used for breaks, and, because Google records when each event arrives, a series of them can show when and how often that happened. A tap on a reminder’s Clear or Snooze 10 min button counts no break and is not sent.

3(a). Data kept on your device

To make the App work, the App stores a small amount of information locally on your device, using Android’s standard app-private SharedPreferences storage. This is the complete list, grouped by what it is for.

Your schedule and reminder (copied by your phone’s backup, below)

  • the days of the week reminders are on, the time they start and end, and how often they come (every 15 to 120 minutes);
  • the sound and the vibration you chose for the reminder;
  • how long each move lasts, as you chose it in Settings (15 seconds to 2 minutes);
  • the reminder text, if you wrote your own. It is shown in the reminder notification, which Android can display on your lock screen;
  • the date and time you ticked the box and tapped Continue on the Your health and safety screen, and the version of the Terms of use shown there (the date at the top of the Terms);
  • the time reminders are paused until, when you pause them: a time later the same day, or the end of the day.

Your breaks (copied by your phone’s backup, below)

  • the number of breaks you finished on each day, for the last 60 days. A break counts when you finish a reset in the App and, in App version 1.0.0, when you press Done on a reminder. Clear and Snooze 10 min on a reminder count no break. The count holds nothing else about a break: not the time, not the moves.

How the reminders run (never copied by the backup)

  • when the last reminder was posted and when the next one is due, and whether Android lets it arrive on the minute (precise timing) or only approximately;
  • how many reminders in a row went unanswered, and, after several, until when the App spaces them out; and which reminder your last finished reset (in App version 1.0.0, also a tap on Done) counted for, so that one reminder never counts twice;
  • when a reminder you snoozed with Snooze 10 min is due to come back, until it comes back or is dropped; and, in App version 1.0.1 and later, the time the next alarm is set for, from which a reminder’s lateness is measured;
  • a record of the last 60 reminder deliveries: for each, when it was due, how late it arrived, what the App decided to do with it, what started it, whether precise timing was in use, and the battery standby group Android had placed the App in. It is shown on a hidden reliability page in the App, so that a late or missing reminder can be explained;
  • the last internal step that failed, with the time and the type of error, and no other detail;
  • the last three reminder lines shown, with the time each was due, so that the next reminder uses different words;
  • a number that names the App’s notification channel, the day the App last showed Android’s notification question, and the last move a break showed, so that the next break starts with the move after it.

Other records (never copied by the backup)

  • whether you own Pro, and when Google Play last confirmed it (Section 3(e));
  • the last copy of the advertising switch file and when it was fetched (Section 3(c));
  • a copy of what Google’s consent component last told the App: whether a consent message applies to you and, if it does, whether it has been answered (not what you answered);
  • when the App last asked you to rate it on Google Play, and the dates it uses to decide when it may ask again (Section 3(f)).

Google’s own components described in Sections 3(b), 3(d) and 7 keep their own records on your device, such as your consent answer and the statistics identifier; those Sections describe them.

This on-device data:

  • is never transmitted to the Developer, who has no servers to receive it;
  • is never sold, and we share it with no one: the only parts that leave your phone are the facts listed in the next point, where the usage statistics are active, and the backup copy described below, which Android makes to your own Google account;
  • contains no account details or identifiers: there is no account, no login, no name, no email address and no device or advertising identifier in it. The one piece of free text is the reminder text you may write yourself, which identifies you only if you put such details in it;
  • is not sent anywhere by the App, except what Section 3(d) describes, only where usage statistics are permitted: how late a reminder arrived (as a band), whether it came at its alarm or was caught up later, whether a reminder was not shown and which of three reasons applied, whether precise timing was in use, the battery standby group, how long a pause you chose lasts (as one of four predefined lengths), your answers to Android’s notification and precise timing questions, and your reminder settings as predefined words: how often reminders come, the sound, whether they vibrate, whether the days are every day, Monday to Friday, Saturday and Sunday or another set, whether the hours are the App’s suggested 09:00 to 18:00 or your own, and whether the reminder text is the App’s or your own. The times you chose, which days make up another set, your reminder text, your count of breaks and the moves you did are never sent;
  • may be included in your phone’s own backup (Android Auto Backup): if backup is switched on in your phone’s settings, Android copies the App’s schedule and reminder settings and its count of breaks to your Google account, and puts them back when you reinstall the App or move to a new phone. Nothing else the App stores is included. That copy is kept by Google under your account, under Google’s terms, and the Developer cannot see it. You can switch backup off in Android’s settings;

Neither deletes the backup copy in your Google account, which Android can put back when the App is installed again, and neither deletes usage statistics already sent (Section 3(d)).

Permissions

The App itself declares four permissions:

  • Notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS), for the break reminder. On Android 13 and later Android asks you first, and the App posts nothing unless you allow it. The App shows Android’s question when you turn reminders on during setup, at most twice and the second time on a later day;
  • Alarms and reminders (SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM), used only if you switch on Precise timing, so that a reminder arrives on the minute. Without it Android may deliver reminders some minutes late;
  • Run at startup (RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED), to restore your reminder schedule after the phone restarts. It sets the next reminder again and reads nothing;
  • Vibration (VIBRATE), to mark each change of move during a reset.

The libraries built into the App, all named in this policy, declare these as well, and only those libraries use them:

  • Internet access (INTERNET) and Network state (ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE), used by the advertising and consent components (3(b)), the single request in 3(c), the usage statistics (3(d)), Google Play’s purchase and rating services (3(e), 3(f)), and by nothing else;
  • Advertising ID (com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID), which allows Google’s advertising component to read the resettable advertising identifier described in 3(b);
  • Ad services (ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION and ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS), Android’s Privacy Sandbox interfaces, declared by Google’s advertising and analytics components, which may use them for the advertising purposes in Section 3(b) under the consent described in Section 7;
  • Keep awake (WAKE_LOCK), which WorkManager, a scheduling library used by Google’s components, uses to finish short pieces of background work. The App’s own reminders do not use it;
  • Google Play billing (com.android.vending.BILLING), which lets the App offer the Pro purchase through Google Play (Section 3(e));
  • Install referrer (com.google.android.finsky.permission.BIND_GET_INSTALL_REFERRER_SERVICE), which lets the App ask Google Play, when it opens, which link brought you to the App (Section 3(d));
  • an internal permission named after the App (com.luxebyte.posture.DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION), declared by Android’s support library so that only the App itself can send messages to its own components.

The App declares no other permission. It has no access to your camera, microphone, location, contacts, files, motion sensors or phone state, and requests no permission to access your name, contacts or account details. Its advertising SDK can access the resettable advertising identifier through the AD_ID permission, subject to your choices and your device settings, as Section 3(b) describes. It never reads your location from the phone; Google can still estimate an approximate location from the IP address its components see (Sections 3(b) and 3(d)).

The reminder. Reminders are scheduled on your device with Android’s alarm service; there is no push service and no server, and nothing is sent to anyone to make them arrive. A reminder never contains an ad. Tapping it opens a short reset, the one screen that can show the banner described in Section 3(b). You can change or stop reminders at any time in the App’s Settings, or in Android’s notification settings for the App.

3(b). Advertising data — collected and shared by Google (AdMob)

The App can show one small banner advertisement, anchored at the bottom of the reset while its moves play. The reset is also the screen a reminder opens. The banner is never shown on the Home screen, on the screen that follows a finished break, during setup, in the schedule, the settings or the list of moves, in a routine, or in a reminder, and it never covers a button, the moving figure or the timer. In the regions where a consent message applies, no ad is requested until you have answered it, and that message never appears during a reset (Section 7).

To serve this ad we use the Google Mobile Ads SDK (Google AdMob). When an ad is requested and shown, Google and its advertising partners collect and process the following categories of data for the purposes of serving ads, measuring ad performance, and detecting and stopping fraud and abuse:

  • the Android Advertising ID (AAID), a resettable advertising identifier;
  • the app-set ID, an identifier scoped to apps from the same developer;
  • your IP address, which Google may use to estimate the general location of the device;
  • technical information about the device (such as device model, operating-system version, language and region, and similar technical data);
  • your interactions with ads (such as whether an ad was shown, viewed, or clicked), and your interactions with the App as Google’s SDK observes them, such as the App’s launch and taps;
  • information about how the App and the SDK perform, such as launch time, how often the App stops responding, and energy use.

This advertising data is collected by and shared with Google and Google’s advertising partners. The Developer does not receive it. Because Google’s advertising infrastructure is global, this data may be processed on servers located outside the European Union / European Economic Area, including in the United States (see Section 6).

Nothing about your breaks is ever sent with an ad request: not your schedule, not your reminder text, not your count of breaks, not the moves you did.

3(c). One request to a static file, so ads can be switched off without an update

The App fetches a single small text file from a set address:

https://matteomark.github.io/ads-flag.txt

The file holds one line per switch, and the App reads only the line saying whether this App’s advertising is on. It exists so that ads can be turned off without publishing a new version of the App.

The App sends nothing with this request: no identifier, no advertising ID, no setting, nothing about you or your breaks. But as with any request to any web server, the server that answers it sees the IP address the request came from, together with ordinary connection information such as the time, the file requested and the type of client. That server is GitHub Pages, operated by GitHub, Inc., which hosts the file as a plain static document. We run no server for it, and we never see a log of these requests.

The App keeps the last copy of the file on your device and fetches it again from time to time while you use the App. If the App has no copy, or its copy is more than 7 days old, it keeps advertising switched off.

Separately from this file, the App asks Google’s consent component, the Google User Messaging Platform, when it starts whether a consent message is required for you (Section 7), because the answer governs both the advertising in Section 3(b) and the usage statistics in Section 3(d). That request goes to Google, which sees the IP address it comes from and ordinary connection information, and decides from them which message, if any, applies. The component keeps your answers on your device, so that the advertising and statistics components can apply them. Outside the EEA, the UK and Switzerland that check usually shows nothing at start; in a US state with a privacy law it shows nothing at start and makes the Privacy choices row appear in Settings, which opens that state’s message (Sections 7 and 9).

3(d). Usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

Why this exists, plainly. Posture Break is made by one person. Android phones delay alarms in different ways, and the only way to learn whether reminders reach people on time, on phones we do not own, is a small, predefined set of facts sent by the App. The same facts show which parts of the App people use, and so whether the App is understood, and the reminder settings people keep show which settings the App should suggest to new users. This Section describes every one of them. They never decide what the App does: the schedule, the moves and the reminders follow rules that read none of this data.

We describe this data as pseudonymous, and deliberately not as “anonymous”. Each installation is identified by a random identifier generated by Google’s SDK, and Google sees the IP address the data arrives from. That is enough for the data to count as personal data, so this policy handles it as personal data throughout.

Where this is active

In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, nothing is sent unless you say yes. The consent message described in Section 7 includes a purpose for these statistics. If you consent to that purpose, the App collects what this Section describes; if you decline it, or do not answer, nothing is sent at all: not a reduced set, nothing. You can change your answer at any time from Settings → Privacy choices; a withdrawal takes effect immediately and discards the identifier described above. Whether a consent message is required for you at all is decided by Google’s consent system, from the message we publish for those regions. The App does not work out your location itself.

Everywhere else, the statistics are on. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in knowing whether reminders arrive on time, whether the App is understood and which settings to suggest (Section 4). You have the right to object at any time (Section 9). The App has no switch of its own for the statistics in those places, and buying Pro does not turn them off. Uninstalling the App stops the collection. Clearing the App’s storage removes the identifier, but collection starts again, under a new identifier, the next time the App runs. Neither deletes what was already sent.

Our Analytics property is linked to AdMob. Our Google Analytics property is linked to our Google AdMob account, so the statistics, including the events Google records by itself (below), are also available in AdMob, for reports on the App’s advertising and for the AdMob features Google offers through that link, under Google’s terms. The link adds nothing to what an individual ad request carries (Section 3(b)). Google’s role, and the data-sharing settings of our property, are described in Section 5.

3(e). Purchases (Posture Break Pro)

Pro is a one-time purchase, made through Google Play. It removes the banner in Section 3(b) and unlocks the extra routines in the App’s library. It does not turn off the usage statistics in Section 3(d), and Google’s consent message still applies where it applies. There is no subscription and no trial.

  • Google Play processes the payment. You pay with the payment method in your Google account, under Google Play’s terms. The App and the Developer never see your card or bank details.
  • What the App receives from Google Play: whether you own Pro, whether a payment is still waiting, and a purchase token, which the App returns to Google Play to confirm the purchase, as Google Play requires. The App holds the token in memory for that confirmation and does not store it. The App keeps on your device only whether you own Pro and when Google Play last confirmed it (Section 3(a)). That record is not copied by your phone’s backup: the App asks Google Play again each time it opens, which also restores Pro when you reinstall the App or use another phone with the same Google account.
  • What the Developer receives: Google Play makes a record of each order available to us in the Google Play Console (for example the order number, the date, the product and the amount), under Google Play’s terms. We use it only for accounting and tax, refunds, and to answer you if you write to us about a purchase.
  • In the usage statistics (Section 3(d)): the App sends no purchase event of its own, but where the statistics are active, Google’s statistics SDK records a completed Pro purchase by itself, with the product, its price, the currency and the quantity, as Section 3(d) describes. Nothing about a purchase is sent with an ad request.

3(f). What the App does NOT do

The App contains no account or sign-in, no crash-reporting service of its own, no attribution SDK other than Google Play’s own install referrer (Section 3(d)), no social features and no server of ours. Google’s advertising SDK (3(b)) and statistics SDK (3(d)) may collect crash and diagnostic information about their own operation, which Google processes as those Sections describe. Apart from the Google advertising and consent components (3(b), 7), the single static-file request (3(c)), the statistics component (3(d)), Google Play’s billing service (3(e)), and Google Play’s rating card (below), the App makes no network requests of any kind.

Rating the App. From time to time, after you have used the App for some days, the App may ask Google Play to show its own rating card. Google Play decides whether it appears, and handles your rating and any review under its own terms. The App is not told whether you rated it, or what you wrote.

3(g). When you write to us

If you write to dev@luxebytelabs.com, we process your email address, your message and anything you choose to include, to answer you, to help with a purchase and to handle a privacy request. The address forwards to a mailbox hosted by Google (Gmail), so Google processes these messages under its terms. We keep a conversation until the matter is settled and no follow-up is likely, and then delete it, except what a purchase record, a dispute or a legal obligation requires us to keep, for as long as that requires.

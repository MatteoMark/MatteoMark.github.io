3. What data is processed

The App processes four separate categories of data: data that remains on your device, apart from Android’s own backup into your Google Drive (Section 3(a)); advertising data, collected by Google and never received by the Developer (Section 3(b)); a single request for a static file, which carries no information about you but necessarily discloses the IP address it is sent from (Section 3(c)); and a limited set of counts and category values describing how reliably the App is working, which is not collected at all in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, and which can be switched off at any time everywhere else (Section 3(d)).

3(a) — Data kept on your device, never transmitted to us

To provide its features, the App stores a small amount of information locally on your device only, using Android’s standard SharedPreferences storage. This includes:

  • your shake counts (how many times the App has detected a shake);
  • your daily streak (consecutive-day usage counter);
  • your achievements (in-app milestones you have unlocked);
  • your block mini-game progress, where that feature is enabled for your device (see Section 3(c)) — your best score, and how many rounds you have finished;
  • your settings and preferences, such as shake sensitivity and the theme (light/dark) you have chosen.

The App does not transmit this data in the form of the values described above, subject only to the two following exceptions: Android’s own backup, described in this Section, and the two coarse bands described in Section 3(d). The data is not transmitted to the Developer, who operates no servers able to receive it, and is not sold or shared with anyone. It remains under your control: uninstalling the App, or clearing the App’s storage/data in Settings → Apps → Shake to Enable Torch → Storage, deletes it.

Android’s own backup. The App leaves Android’s standard Auto Backup enabled, which is the platform default. If device backup is turned on in your Android settings, Android — not the App — copies the App’s stored preferences to your own Google Drive, and includes them when you transfer to a new phone. That copy is not limited to the list above: it includes every preference the App holds on the device, which also covers the cached copy of the file described in Section 3(c) and the preferences written by Google’s advertising and consent components — among them the record of the advertising-consent choice you made in the consent form described in Section 7. The copy leaves your device and is held by Google under your own Google account, and it is the one way the data in this Section 3(a) is transmitted at all. The Developer cannot see it, reach it, or request it: app backups are not accessible to app developers, we operate no servers, and we receive nothing from it. The backup is controlled by you alone — you can turn backup off, or delete this App’s backup, in Settings → Google → Backup.

One narrow carve-out applies. Where the usage and reliability statistics described in Section 3(d) are active — never in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, and elsewhere only until you turn the in-app switch off — two of the values above are also sent as coarse bands, never as the values themselves: the quarter of the sensitivity slider you are on, and approximately how many mini-game rounds you have finished. Your best score, your shake counts, your streak and your achievements are not sent in any form. Apart from Android’s backup and that carve-out, nothing in this Section 3(a) leaves your device.

The App also reads your device’s accelerometer (motion sensor) through a foreground service in order to detect shaking. Where the device provides them, it additionally registers the proximity, tilt, wake-gesture and pick-up-gesture sensors (or a manufacturer’s equivalent of them). These sensors are used only as a signal that the device has been picked up or moved, so that shake detection can be restarted after Android has suspended it; they are not used to observe you. The App does not use the proximity sensor to determine where the device is or what is near it.

All of these readings are processed transiently on the device to decide whether to toggle the flashlight; they are not recorded, stored, profiled, or transmitted. No sample, trace or magnitude of any of them leaves your device in any form. Two values derived from them can: the shake counts and streak listed above leave by the Android backup route described immediately above — into your own Google Drive, never to the Developer — and, where the statistics in Section 3(d) are enabled, what may be sent is a banded count of how many shakes were detected during a window and a three-way comparison of the strongest reading in that window against the shake threshold you chose (below / near / above) — never a sample, never a trace, never a magnitude. Nothing that leaves your device could reconstruct your movement.

If you enable the option to stop the torch during phone calls, the App requests the Android phone-state permission (android.permission.READ_PHONE_STATE) and then observes whether a call is ringing, in progress, or finished, and nothing more. It does not read your phone number, your call history, your contacts, or who is calling. The state is read transiently on the device, for the sole purpose of not leaving a torch on during a call, and is never profiled or transmitted to the Developer.

So that the torch can be restored to its previous state, the App stores on the device the time at which a call-related pause began. That timestamp is kept in the same preferences described above, and is therefore included in the Android backup described above. It records nothing about the call itself — no number, no direction, no duration, no identity — and it is never sent to the Developer.

The permission is requested at the moment you enable that option, not at install time, and the feature is optional: if you refuse the permission, or leave the option off, the rest of the App is unaffected. You can withdraw the permission at any time in Settings → Apps → Shake to Enable Torch → Permissions.

So that shake detection keeps working on devices whose manufacturer shuts background apps down, the App registers a device-local account named “Shake to Torch (keep-alive)”, visible in Settings → Accounts. It holds no credentials and no data, it is not linked to any online account, and nothing is ever synchronised through it: Android’s sync scheduler is used solely as a periodic prompt for the App to check whether its own flashlight service is still running. Uninstalling the App removes it.

3(c) — One request to a static file, so ads can be switched off without an update

The App fetches a single small text file from a fixed address:

https://matteomark.github.io/ads-flag.txt

It does so at most once a day. The App keeps its own copy of the file and re-uses that copy for a day before requesting a fresh one, so opening the App repeatedly does not repeat the request.

The lines in that file state whether advertising is enabled at all, and which of the App’s optional features are enabled individually — the dark-theme rewarded ad, the block mini-game, and the promotion of our other app. The file exists so that any of them can be switched off without publishing a new version of the App. It does not govern the usage and reliability statistics described in Section 3(d); those are governed only by the controls listed there.

The App sends nothing with this request: no identifier, no advertising ID, and no information about you or about how you use the App. As with any request to any web server, the server that answers it necessarily sees the IP address the request came from, together with ordinary connection information such as the time, the file requested and the type of client. That server is GitHub Pages, operated by GitHub, Inc., which hosts the file as a plain static document. The Developer runs no server and never sees a log of these requests.

3(d) — Usage and reliability statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

A large share of the negative reviews the App receives report the same fault: the shake stops working. The App cannot otherwise determine why on any device except the Developer’s own — whether the background service was terminated by the phone’s manufacturer, whether the hardware has no usable motion sensor, or whether the shake was detected but the flashlight failed to come on. Those are three different faults with three different remedies, and these statistics exist so that the cause can be identified across many devices rather than one.

This data is described as pseudonymous, not as anonymous: each installation is identified by a random identifier generated by Google’s SDK, and Google sees the IP address the data arrives from. That is sufficient for the data to constitute personal data, and it is treated as personal data throughout this policy.

Where this is active, and where nothing is sent at all

The territorial position is set out first.

In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, nothing is sent: no reduced set, no anonymised set, and no data of this category in any form. The consent form described in Section 7 is presented by a Google component that, in the version this release ships, carries no analytics purpose, so the App can never receive consent for this purpose in those regions and treats it as never given. That is a property of the App as built. It rests on one matter that is not: whether a consent form is required for you at all is decided by Google’s consent system, on the basis of the consent message we publish for those regions — the same mechanism that already decides whether the advertising described in Section 3(b) is personalised there. The App does not determine your location itself, and does not attempt to. Should either of those change, that form becomes the control, this Section will be amended accordingly, and this policy will be updated before any collection begins there.

Everywhere else, these statistics are active. The App carries the Google Analytics for Firebase SDK and is built with the Google project credentials it needs, so what is described below is a collection and not merely a capability. Collection is on unless you turn it off: the in-app “share usage statistics” switch starts in the on position, and turning it off stops the collection immediately and completely and also discards the identifier described further down, so that switching it back on later starts a new one rather than resuming the old. Section 4 sets out the legal basis, which outside those regions is our legitimate interest in diagnosing a fault we otherwise cannot see, with that opt-out available at all times.

  • how many shakes were detected, how many flashlight commands were attempted, how many failed, and how many were refused by the camera — each as a band (“0”, “1–5”, “6–20”, “more than 20”);

  • roughly how many rounds you have finished, as a band;
  • when the occasional startup prompt is shown — the offer of our other app, which is the only such prompt the App shows. It is counted once when it appears and not counted again until you answer it, so a screen that Android rebuilds is not counted twice;
  • how that prompt ended, as one of four fixed values: you accepted it and went to the store listing; you tried the demo instead; you chose “not now”; or you dismissed it with the back gesture. If you never answer it, nothing is sent;

  • when one of the three features that sit behind an optional rewarded video is unlocked — the dark theme, the achievements page, or an extra life in the block mini-game — which of the three it was, and why it was granted, as one of four fixed values: you watched the ad; it was granted free because you had watched one in the last ten minutes, the App not asking twice in quick succession; it was granted free because ads could not be served on your device at that moment at all, advertising being switched off or consent refused; or it was granted free because the ad tried to load or to show and failed. These named controls are not the only interactions recorded: opening the mini-game, the startup prompt and your answer to it, opening the store listing, and unlocking a feature behind a rewarded video are each recorded exactly as the corresponding point above describes, and no further. Taken together, the points in this list are the complete record of what you touch, and a control that none of them describes is deliberately not recorded at all. There are no coordinates and no session recording.

The four items above concerning the startup prompt, its outcome, the store listing, and the rewarded-video unlocks carry only the fixed values listed with them: no free text, no timing figure, no reading from any sensor, no information about where you are, and no identifier of their own. What is deliberately not collected

By design, none of the following is collected:

  • No raw accelerometer readings, traces or magnitudes ever leave your device; only the coarse band comparisons described above do.
  • No advertising ID is used for this purpose; the App instructs the SDK not to collect it.   • No information from the phone-state / call-state check; that feature stays entirely on your device (Section 3(a)).

  • No memory figures, and no precise timestamps as event fields.
